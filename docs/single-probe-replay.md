# Selecting an adaptive policy without measuring imaginary latency

A split model can inspect its native legal-slot probabilities after the first middle-block visit. If the two leading probabilities differ by at least a fixed threshold, it can return that first result. Otherwise it continues from the saved hidden state to a fixed cap, **without inspecting the intermediate exits**, then evaluates the coda once more. No label, cached choice, or replay may drive a live decision.

The development design in `configs/evaluation/one-probe.json` uses completed six-pass diagnostic streams for both original payloads. `probe_replay.load` binds the probabilities to the packet, ordered input IDs and hashes, the six actually recorded codas and block visits, plus the matching returned endpoint. A diagnostic trace that recorded only the final readout cannot be used. `partition` deterministically separates whole graphs within each routing task. Pass only fit graphs and labels to `fit`, persist its answer for **both** payloads, then pass the separate graphs and labels to `audit`. A failing rule is not replaced with another one after the audit.

For a physical decoder of depth `D` and the middle window `[8,12)`, an early exit visits `D` layers. A continuation to `k` visits `D + 4*(k-1) + (D-12)` layers: the last term is the second coda. This is a logical work count, not elapsed time, a hardware FLOP count, or the latency of a future implementation. A replayed policy and a live actuation must have distinct provenance. The official one-pass cached path also does not have the split path's latency.
